Historic Sites / Hamams

Aydınoğlu Mehmet Bey Hamam

Çarşı (Market) Hamam / Birgivi Hamam

Show on map

It stands at the edge of the area where Aydınoğlu Fatih Mehmet Bey Street and İmam-ı Birgivi Street meet, between the Ulu Cami and the Birgivi Madrasa. The hamam is reported to have been built in the 15th century. Today only the east and south façades of the building can be seen. The hamam is no longer in use, and its changing room (soyunmalık) no longer exists. Its four large domes cover the bathing halls. The transition to the dome circles is made with squinches. Round openings in the domes let light into the hamam. The floor is paved with marble. Three of the halls have fountains, now dry, with marble basins (kurna) below them. One basin bears a wheel-of-fortune (çarkıfelek) motif and another palmette motifs. In one hall a marble tub has been placed beside the marble basin. Behind the hamam is its furnace (külhan), which is no longer usable.
