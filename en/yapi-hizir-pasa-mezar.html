Grave of Hızır Paşa

Show on map

Celâleddin Hızır (Hacı Paşa), originally from Konya and thought to have been born after 740 AH (1339), went to Egypt to study and read the religious and rational sciences with the famous Hanafi jurist Ekmeleddin el-Bâbertî at the Şeyhûniyye Madrasa in Cairo, where his sharp mind and diligence won his teacher's praise. His success there also made his name known in Anatolia. A serious illness he suffered as a student in Cairo led Hacı Paşa to take up medicine. Alongside his religious studies he trained himself in medicine by reading medical books and learning from the knowledge and experience of physicians such as Cemâleddin İbnü'ş-Şevbekî, and, as he himself states in the preface of his work Şifâʾü'l-esḳām, he began to work as a physician at the hospital (bîmâristân) of el-Melikü'l-Mansûr Kalavun. There is no doubt that Hacı Paşa, who besides serving as a judge (kadı) taught at the madrasas of Ayasuluk and Birgi and worked as a court physician, was held in high esteem by the ruler and the court as a Turkish physician and was loved by the people. He began writing his greatest work, his Qur'an commentary (tefsir), after the accession of Murad II (824/1421) and dedicated it to him; it appears that he then returned to Birgi, the town of learning he loved so much, and died there shortly afterwards. The exact date of his death is not known. In 1935 a marble monument was built over Hacı Paşa's grave at the place called Hıdırlık in Birgi. Best known for his works on medicine, Hacı Paşa also wrote valuable works on Qur'anic commentary, theology (kelâm) and logic; apart from two medical books in Turkish, he wrote all his works in Arabic.
