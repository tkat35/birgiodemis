Historic Sites / Other Historic Buildings

Evliya Çelebi House

Show on map

Located in the Taşpazar area of Birgi, the building is registered as an example of civil architecture in Birgi's conservation-oriented development plan. It is said to be one of the houses where Evliya Çelebi stayed during his visit to Birgi, which is why it is known locally as the Evliya Çelebi House. The two-storey building was built with a barn, a stone-paved hall (taşlık) and a store on the ground floor and living quarters above. Old photographs show that its open hall (sofa), built in an open-hall plan and later destroyed, projected over the street. Evliya Çelebi came to Birgi in the second half of the 17th century. He wrote that Birgi had a castle at the foot of Bozdağ whose outer walls were in ruins, with 400 houses inside the castle; that the lower town had eighteen neighbourhoods, twenty-four mosques and masjids, some 2,600 houses, seven primary schools (sıbyan mektebi), 200 shops, two inns and a covered market (bedesten); and that the people's main livelihood was weaving white thread. It is said that when he came to Birgi he stayed for a while as a guest at the mansions of Soğancızade and Arif Çelebi. While staying at Arif Çelebi's mansion, he received the townspeople and listened to their problems. Next to this mansion, a cupboard in the kitchen of a mansion belonging to one of Arif Çelebi's sons, used for serving food to guests, caught his attention. Evliya Çelebi is said to have written these lines on the back of the door of the room where he stayed:

I have seen many lands,

But never such a cupboard,

This house is such a house that

All its affairs are honey and joy,

May those within never suffer hardship,

Let whoever comes to this house

Eat bread and food;

If he does not pray,

Let him eat earth and stone.
