Historic Sites / Mosques

Dervişağa Mosque

Show on map

It is also called the “Çarşı (Market) Mosque”. According to its inscription, it was built in 1663–64 by El-Hac Derviş Ağa, son of the merchant Hacı Ali and one of the wealthy men of Birgi. Derviş Ağa also built a Dar al-Hadith (madrasa) in Birgi and had the Ulu Cami in Tire repaired and made usable again. The mosque was burned by the Greek army during the Turkish War of Independence but was later rebuilt. In 1939 it was damaged in a flood when the Birgi stream overflowed, and it has survived to the present day thanks to various repairs. The mosque is a square building with a single dome. The large central dome is supported by eight arches. Niches are opened in the corners of the square structure, showing an effort to create more space inside. The late-comers' portico (son cemaat yeri) has a row of three arches on four columns. The column capitals are Corinthian. The portico has three domes: the middle dome is wide and the side domes narrow, giving the building a sense of harmony. The entrance door is a fine example of the well-established Ottoman art of wood carving. The minaret is a slender cylindrical structure with a single balcony, set on a low angular base. The monumental main dome rests on an octagonal base; its weight is lightened by niches opening into the corners of the dome inside and by relieving arches outside. With its late-comers' portico, its minaret, its relieving arches, its monumental dome and portico domes covered in lead, and its pointed and ogee arches, the Derviş Ağa Mosque is one of the fine examples of Ottoman architecture in Anatolia.
