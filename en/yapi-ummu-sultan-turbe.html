Historic Sites / Tombs

Ümmü Sultan Tomb

Show on map

Also known as Hatuniye, Sultanşah and Hanzade Hatun, the tomb stands on Fatih Mehmet Bey Street, just southeast of the Ulu Cami. The Arabic inscription above its entrance states that it was completed in 710 AH (1310/11 AD). Belonging to the group of tombs with a hexagonal prism-shaped body, the tomb has a special place among the tombs of the Aydınid period because of this design, since square and octagonal bodies are common in the other tombs of this principality. The hexagonal body is found only in this building. The tomb is entered from the south through an iwan-type doorway. The hexagonal interior is covered by a dome, the transition to which is made with small muqarnas squinches. Outside, it is covered by a slightly flattened outer dome. The tomb receives light through two windows each on its east and west sides. Each face of the hexagonal burial chamber has shallow niches. The narrow front face has the character of a plain portal. The iwan entrance has a pointed arch, while the entrance to the burial chamber has a flattened arch. Buildings with hexagonal prism-shaped bodies are found in Central and Western Anatolia. The Sultanşah Tomb is the earliest example of this group and pioneered a new tomb design in the period of the Anatolian principalities.
