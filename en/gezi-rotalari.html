Bademli

With its important historic buildings and picnic spots, Bademli has so many places to visit and see that you too will be amazed at how much this small town has to offer. Do not forget to bring your camera. The history of Bademli goes back to 1327. It is written that the name Bademli, also known from İbrahim Bahadır of the Beylik of Aydın being called the 'Bey of Bodamya', comes from 'Potamia', meaning 'land of rivers'. Over time the name Potamia became 'Bademye' in Evliya Çelebi's Book of Travels (Seyahatname), and in 1965 it was changed to Bademli. You can see houses like those of Bademli, which reflect the beauty of old Turkish architecture, only in Birgi. Like the houses of Safranbolu, the houses of Bademli have managed to keep their character. These two-storey houses are built in the “Hanay” style, with a “çardak” (gallery) facing the inner courtyard in front of the rooms built side by side. Be sure to see the castle of 'Potomia'. The parks and picnic spots of Bademli offer a unique place to be alone with nature.

Bozdağ

Once, Tmolos, ruler of the Kingdom of Lydia, went up the mountain to hunt. There he met the water nymph Arripe and fell in love with her. But the goddess Artemis sent a bull to stop the mortal Tmolos's love for the immortal Arripe. Tmolos, killed by the bull, was buried on this mountain by his son, and the mountain came to be called Tmolos. In ancient times, above Hypaipa (Günlüce), on the slopes of this very Mount Tmolos, there was a small settlement around an important temple of Zeus called Dios Hieron. Today this town is known as Bozdağ. This impressive mountain, with its steep cliffs, lush forests and magnificent views, is a natural wonder. It offers adventure lovers activities such as climbing, paragliding and mountaineering, and nature lovers a peaceful escape. Tmolos, or Bozdağ as it is called today, is 2,150 metres high and, with its varied soils and rocks, is home to 120 endemic plants. Fourteen of these endemic taxa are found only on this mountain. Do not leave Ödemiş without seeing this mountain and its town, beautiful in each of the four seasons.

Gölcük

The Gölcük plateau (yayla) on Bozdağ has been an important summer retreat from the time of the Beylik of Aydın to the present day. In the period of the principalities, while Birgi was the winter capital, the Gölcük plateau was used as a kind of summer capital and gained importance. Historical sources likewise tell us that in the Ottoman period the princes residing in Manisa went up to the Gölcük plateau in summer and spent a few months there. Thanks to its many natural and human attractions — its location, transport links, superb views of varied landscapes, climate, water sources, forests, and summer-pasture and farming activities — Lake Gölcük and its plateau form one of the important tourist areas of Western Anatolia.

There are many facilities on the lakeshore, such as hotels, guesthouses, summer houses and tea gardens, to meet the needs of day and overnight visitors. The lake, about 1,050 metres above sea level, was formed by tectonic movements.

Ödemiş

The Ödemiş plain, home to the oldest human culture in Western Anatolia, is a district that is attractive for its natural beauty as well as its history and culture. In this land of the Efes (Efeler diyarı), where the first shot of resistance was fired during the Greek occupation, the economy is based mainly on agriculture and livestock. At the Ödemiş market, held on Saturdays and one of the important markets of the region, you will find not only natural produce but also, in the 'Handicrafts Market' section, local handmade products such as lace, needle lace, shuttle lace, cross-stitch and Ödemiş silk. On the Ödemiş plain, where settlement goes back to around 4000 BC, there are many places to discover and many things to do. To get to know the culture, social life and history of Ödemiş and the Aegean region a little better, you can visit the Ödemiş Museum, the Bedia Akartürk Museum and the Yıldız City Archive and Museum. Have the famous Töngül pide for breakfast, and during the day try Ödemiş köfte (kebab) and other local delicacies — enjoy!

Efeler Yolu (Efeler Way)

Efeler Yolu is a 500-kilometre, multi-stage, waymarked hiking trail that starts in the Bornova district of İzmir and ends at the House of the Virgin Mary (Meryemana) in Ephesus-Selçuk. It is a cultural route built around the efe and zeybek tradition and consists of 28 stages: 27 main stages and 1 alternative line. In 2023 the trail was included in the Green Destinations “Top 100 Stories”.

Birgi is where two stages of the trail meet: stage 10 runs from Gölcük to Birgi, and stage 11 from Birgi to Bozdağ. Stages are between 14 and 24 kilometres long and are generally rated moderate-to-difficult or difficult. The main stages are waymarked in red and white, the alternative line in red and yellow.

Up-to-date GPX and KMZ track files for all stages can be downloaded from efeleryolu.com, and the trail can also be followed with apps such as Wikiloc or AllTrails. Before setting out on these long and demanding stages, check the latest track file and the weather forecast.
