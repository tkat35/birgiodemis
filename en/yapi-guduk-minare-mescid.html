Historic Sites / Other Historic Buildings

Güdük Minare Masjid

Show on map

Owned by the İzmir Regional Directorate of Foundations and known locally as the Mosque with the Stumpy (Güdük or Kütük) Minaret, this historic building stands on steeply sloping ground, so it has one storey on the road side and two when seen from below. The building, now in ruins, is thought from its architectural features to date from the 14th century. It was ruined during the Greek occupation of 1920–1922: as the Greek forces withdrew from Birgi, the town was burned from end to end and many houses and buildings of artistic value were lost. The Güdük Minare Mosque was also badly damaged in this destruction. Today only its north wall and minaret are standing; it was a square building. The minaret consists of a short shaft on a five-sided base, and its balcony parapet is also of brick. The masjid, built of rubble and stone, is architecturally plain. It is entered through a rectangular door in the north wall, next to the minaret, opening onto the late-comers' portico. The wooden floor in the southwest corner has collapsed, while the lower-floor entrance and windows are still standing.
