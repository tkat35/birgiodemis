Historic Sites / Madrasas

Dervişağa Madrasa

Show on map

Standing at the corner where Kazım Dirik Street meets Okul Street, opposite the Derviş Ağa Hamam, the madrasa was built by Derviş Ağa in 1657. It has an open courtyard with a well. Also known as the Dar al-Hadith and the Çukur (Sunken) Madrasa, and restored in 2012, the madrasa has an L-shaped plan, with five of its cells in the west wing and two in the south wing. There is also a classroom (dershane) in the southwest corner. The cells, built of cut slate (kayrak) stone, marble, brick and rubble stone, are covered with small domes, and the classroom with a central dome. Stone and brick are laid in alternating courses in the walls. The piers in front of the cells are connected by round arches, and inside the cells there are niches and fireplaces. The cells have windows facing the courtyard. The classroom has a square base and is covered with a dome on a drum. It is entered through a door with a round arch and a stone lintel.
