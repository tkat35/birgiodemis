Historic Sites / Hamams

Dervişağa Hamam

Çukur (Sunken) Hamam / Şeyh Muhiddin Hamam

Show on map

The construction inscription of the Derviş Ağa Hamam has not survived. It stands opposite the Derviş Ağa Madrasa, with its façade facing the madrasa (the Çukur Madrasa). Archaeological excavations have revealed a marble pavement between the two buildings. The hamam consists of private bathing cells (halvet) arranged around a square hot room. Although some sources call it the Şeyh Muhiddin Hamam or the Derviş Ağa Hamam, it is known locally as the Çukur (Sunken) Hamam. Although there is no information on when it was built, its plan and structure show 15th-century architectural features. According to archival records, the hamam was endowed to the zawiya (dervish lodge) of Şeyh Muhiddin bin İsmail and is a double hamam known as the Sarı Bey Hamam. The hamam is built of cut stone with brick bands and has a rectangular plan. It consists of a changing room, a warm room and a hot room, each covered by a dome on pendentives.
