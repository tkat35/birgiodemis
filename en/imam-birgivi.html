İmam-ı Birgivi

İmam-ı Birgivi was a scholar who lived in the period when the Ottoman Empire reached its greatest extent. Mehmet Efendi received his madrasa education in Istanbul. At the request of Ataullah Atai Ahmet Efendi, he wrote the treatise “Cilaü'l-kulub”. In Birgi he taught at the Dar al-Hadith madrasa, led prayers in the mosque and preached to the people, and in the rest of his time he wrote his books. His reputation as a scholar later spread across the country, and students began to flock to his madrasa in Birgi to learn. In the last years of his life, Birgivi Mehmet Efendi went to Istanbul to give advice on correcting a number of wrong practices he saw in religious affairs and in the government, and met the Grand Vizier of the time, Sokullu Mehmet Paşa. While he was teaching, reconciling people, showing them the right path, giving advice and writing books in Birgi, he died in a plague epidemic on 21 September 1573.

In Istanbul, Birgivi Mehmet Efendi studied with Küçük Şemseddin Efendi, professor at the Mahmut Paşa Madrasa. He later became a student of Abdurrahman Efendi, professor at the Haseki Madrasa. He also learned from Ahizade Karamani Mehmet Efendi, another well-known professor of the time. Mehmet Efendi then became a candidate (mülazım) under Abdurrahman Efendi, the chief military judge (kazasker) of Rumelia. After leaving his post as kassam-ı askeri (a judicial official dividing estates), Mehmet Efendi joined Karamanlı Abdullah Efendi, one of the sheikhs of the Bayramiye order in Istanbul, to follow the Sufi way.

When he came to Birgi, he was appointed professor at the Dar al-Hadith Madrasa with a daily salary of 60 akçe. His teaching at his madrasa in Birgi made him famous throughout the country, and students began to flock to his madrasa to study. Birgivi Mehmet Efendi described the features and life of the town of Birgi in his treatise “Avalu etfai'l-müslimin”.

İmam Birgivi Mehmet Efendi's father, Ali Efendi, was a professor with an interest in Sufism. When Mehmet Efendi finished his early education, he also taught logic. By the time he completed his early education, Mehmet Efendi had also memorised the Holy Qur'an. Pir Ali Efendi, who sent his son to Istanbul to continue his education, died in Balıkesir and is buried in the cemetery in the Çay neighbourhood.

In Birgi are the madrasa, mosque and tomb of İmam Birgivi Mehmet Efendi. His grave is here and has become an important place of pilgrimage in the Islamic world. People come to his grave from the surrounding provinces and districts by various means of transport. These visits, a form of faith tourism, are most frequent in spring and autumn.

Birgivi Mehmet Efendi had no patience with corruption and fought against it in religious affairs, in government and in private life. To see justice done, he even gave advice to the Grand Vizier Sokullu Mehmet Paşa. He argued for the strict application of Islamic rules and opposed the use of the Holy Qur'an for personal gain. At the same time, he devoted his life to showing people the right path and giving them advice. He was a learned, virtuous and mature person. He used reason in his conduct and believed that the most correct behaviour was to bring his love of God to a more mature form through Sufism. His books attracted the attention of the scholarly circles of his time, and he was known for his moral, moderate and virtuous character. With works reflecting the social life of his time, he held a distinctive place among Ottoman writers. In short, Birgivi Mehmet Efendi was one of the great religious scholars raised by the Turks in the 16th century.
